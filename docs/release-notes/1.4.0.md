# Neon Work 1.4.0 — release notes

**Planning**: a lightweight project planner over the tasks already in your project notes. Nothing changes in a note until you act, and read-only still blocks every write. Ideas from the Project Planner plugin (Harrison Milburn, MIT); no code taken.

## Plans in your notes

- **Tasks as you write them for the Tasks plugin**, plus three things: an id (`🆔 sdr-4`), what it waits for (`⛔ sdr-2,sdr-3`) and an estimate in working days (`[duration:: 3d]`, or `1w`). A task without an estimate counts as one day.
- **Milestones** are tasks tagged `#milestone`; **`#blocked`** marks something held up outside the plan; **`#risk`, `#issue`, `#decision`** mark a task; **owners** are `[owner:: [[@Sam Taylor]], Alex]`.
- **Which tasks belong to a project:** those in its note, or in every note of a project folder, plus tasks elsewhere linked to them by a dependency (shown as "outside this project").
- **States:** ready (nothing open before it), **waiting** (earlier tasks open but on time), **blocked** (a task upstream is overdue or `#blocked`; the cause is named), in progress, done.

## The Planning view

- A **Planning** button on every project note (and *Project: open Planning*, and the project's menu) opens the plan in its own tab, named *Planning: <note>*.
- **Gantt** (drawn by the plugin, in your theme's colours): phases as sections, the critical path outlined, milestones as diamonds, a line for today, a *Project* section with the forecast finish and the due date. *Dates* (from the project's `started` date) or *Weeks*; *Tasks* or *Phases* (one bar per phase); *SVG* and *Copy as Mermaid*.
- **Board:** Ready, Waiting, In progress, Done, rows by phase or by person. Drag a card (or use its menu) to change its checkbox; done adds ✅ today.
- **Graph:** the dependencies, top to bottom or across, with phase groups if you like; thick arrows on the critical path.
- **List:** state, owner, id, dependencies, days (− and + to change), start, finish, slack, and slip against a saved baseline; filter to risks, issues or decisions.
- **One set of colours everywhere:** ready blue, waiting pale blue, blocked purple, in progress cyan, due within a week orange, overdue red, done green. Risks and issues are bold with a dashed outline.
- **Warnings:** a task planned to finish after its 📅 date, due before a task it waits for, or in progress past its planned finish (⏰ on its row in the List); someone with more than five days of work in a week; dates on a weekend. A person filter shows one person's tasks.
- **Report** saves a printable snapshot of the plan in the reports folder (*Settings → Projects → Planning reports folder*; empty: `<base folder>/Reports`): a note with the summary and forecast, the Gantt chart (an SVG in the plan's colours), phases, what needs attention, risks / issues / decisions, the critical path, workload and every task. Print it with *Export to PDF*. A second report the same day replaces that day's.
- **Dates…** writes the plan's dates into the tasks (🛫 start, 📅 due), for the tasks you tick; tasks that already have dates are left unticked.
- **Forecast finish** against the project's `due` date (on time, or N working days late), and a **baseline** to see how far tasks slip: saved automatically the first time the project is in progress (with a started date and tasks) and provisional — it follows every change — until every open task has a duration, then locked; kept in the plugin's own data (no files), following a renamed or moved project and dropped when the project is gone; **Re-baseline** or remove it from the Planning tab's ⋯ menu.

## Priority

- The Tasks plugin's priorities (🔺 highest, ⏫ high, 🔼 medium, 🔽 low, ⏬ lowest) show on board cards, Gantt rows and in a Priority column of the List (which can also order each phase by priority). Board columns put the highest first. They don't change the schedule: dependencies and durations do.
- The task editor sets them; when someone has too much work in a week, the warning names the task easiest to move (lowest priority, then most slack).
- The Tasks card orders tasks with the same date by priority and shows the emoji.

## The task editor

- Click a task in any view (or right-click it, or *Planning: edit this task* in the note's own right-click menu) for a form: description, status, phase, owners, duration or milestone, `#blocked`, markers, what it depends on and what it blocks (loops can't be made), dates with the plan's own dates, and the id, given automatically (the `plan-prefix` property, else the title's initials: `pla-12`).
- A preview shows exactly what will be written, including changes to other tasks. Fields it doesn't manage stay where they are.
- **New task** follows the selected task: it depends on it and goes straight after its line, so a chain is quick to build.

## Elsewhere

- **Tasks in projects** are left off the Tasks card and tab (setting *Tasks in projects*); each project's row says how its tasks stand instead: overdue, due this week, blocked, ready, in progress, waiting, done, and the forecast.
- **Projects card:** two tidy lines per project, with small pills for what needs action (late, overdue, due soon, blocked).
- **Projects timeline: all projects in one view.** One row per project, from its start to its plan's forecast finish, a diamond on the due date, coloured late, needs attention, in progress, not started, on hold or closed. A click opens the project's Planning view. The scale labels months when weeks would crowd.
- **Tasks timeline** is drawn by the plugin too, coloured overdue, due within a week, in progress or open; a click opens the task.
- **Card titles open their tab:** Inbox, Tasks, Todo, Projects and Time; the expand icons are gone.
- **Plan template:** `project_plan.md` in the sample vault's templates, four phases of example tasks; New project fills its `{{prefix}}` ids, and *Fill in ids* in the Planning view does it for notes made another way.
- **Sample vault:** *Service Desk Rollout* (Globex) shows every state, owners, markers, a milestone and dependencies in and out of the project.

## Good to know

- Obsidian now draws ```` ```mermaid ```` blocks with its own engine (Pele), so charts pasted with *Copy as Mermaid* show in Obsidian's colours, not the Planning view's.
- The Tasks plugin's `is blocked` filter means what Planning calls *waiting*.
- No hotkey is set by default; give *Project: open Planning* one in *Settings → Hotkeys* (for example Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+G).

## Fixes

- The Planning report's chart (and the SVG export) is a valid file again: theme font names put double quotes inside the SVG's style.
- Tabs left as "neon-deck-…" placeholders after switching the plugin off and on are reopened once their module starts.
- With customer folders the "No category yet" bar no longer appears; it is off by default, and vaults that saved it on get it switched off once.
- Console messages, the Time ribbon button and comments name Neon Work (Neon Deck only as the origin).

- An empty “Project folders” setting no longer turns every top-level folder (such as 2.Resources) into a project.

## Help

- A **?** button at the top of every Neon Work tab opens help for that part inside Obsidian (it works offline): the README's section, with a row of topics to move between, links to its pictures on GitHub and a link to the full page. The build cuts the help from the README, so the two always agree.

## Housekeeping

- About 100 unused style rules removed (old ticket templates and screenshots, the old research card, the Write toolbar and slash menu); no visible change.
- Corner radii in the Dashboard use theme tokens (new `--nd-radius-xs` and `--nd-radius-pill`).
- The Dashboard scans the vault once per redraw instead of once per card.
- Long source files split: `shared.js` into three, the pattern shaders into their own file, and the long stylesheets by section.
